Red-flagResearch

Live, public screening for automated-research attacks on peer review

Red-flag Research is a research project from OpenLAIR that screens conference submissions for automated-research attacks, starting with paper laundering in ICLR 2027. It checks each submission against earlier public papers and flags close matches that lack clear credit, so a person can compare the two side by side. A flag is a lead, not a verdict.

Public: benchmark, evaluation tools and baseline outputs. Detection pipeline code: not released yet.

A flag is a lead

What a label means, what it does not mean, and how to get a result corrected.

A lead for review, not a verdict

Red-flag Research is a research project that screens conference submissions automatically. A Source review recommended label means the system found passages, numbers or tables that closely match an earlier public paper without a clear attribution, so a person should compare the two papers side by side.

It is not a finding of plagiarism, misconduct or bad faith. It has not been reviewed by the conference, the authors or any integrity committee. The system uses language models and can be wrong: shared authors, an earlier version of the same work, a cited dataset or a common baseline can all explain matching text, and the system may not see them. A paper without a label has not been cleared either.

Benchmark cases are anonymized before release.

Result labels

  • Source review recommendedClose matches to an earlier public paper without clear attribution. A person should compare the two.
  • No source findingNothing found in the sources the system checked. This does not clear the paper.
  • InconclusiveNot enough evidence for either label.
  • Same paper (preprint)The closest match is this paper's own earlier preprint.

Think a result is wrong? If you are an author and believe a result about your paper is wrong, tell us through the feedback form or at red.flag.autoresearch@gmail.com. We will correct it right away, and we take the result off the live page and the public data while we check.

Questions

Short answers about what the project does, what is public, and how to get a result corrected.

What is Red-flag Research?

Red-flag Research is a research project from OpenLAIR that screens conference submissions for automated-research attacks, starting with paper laundering in ICLR 2027. It was started by Ziqian Zhang at OpenLAIR (Lehigh AI Research Lab) in October 2026.

What is paper laundering?

Paper laundering is rewriting earlier public work into a submission that looks new, without credit to the original. Language models make the rewriting cheap, and text-similarity checkers often miss it because the wording changes while claims, numbers and tables stay close to the original.

Is Red-flag Research a plagiarism checker?

Not in the usual sense. Similarity checkers such as iThenticate look for copied wording. Red-flag Research compares claims, passages, numbers and tables with earlier public papers even after rewording, and it never decides whether anything is plagiarism. A person does.

What does “Source review recommended” mean?

It means the system found passages, numbers or tables that closely match an earlier public paper without a clear attribution, so a person should compare the two papers. It is not a finding of misconduct and has not been reviewed by the conference, the authors or any integrity committee.

Which papers does it screen, and when?

The first application screens all 43,013 ICLR 2027 submissions in nine public stages of up to 5,000 papers. Stage 1 starts on October 16, 2026 at 9 PM US Eastern, and the run aims to finish before ICLR 2027 decisions on December 16, 2026.

What is publicly available?

The benchmark (20 anonymized ICLR 2027 cases with human labels), the evaluation tools and the saved baseline outputs are open at github.com/OpenLAIR/red-flag-research. The detection pipeline code is not released yet.

Is Red-flag Research affiliated with ICLR?

No. It is an independent research project from OpenLAIR. It uses submission metadata that is public on OpenReview and does not host papers.

How do I get a result corrected?

Use the feedback form on the site or e-mail red.flag.autoresearch@gmail.com. The result comes off the live page and the public data while we check, and we correct it right away if it is wrong.
